1 Abstract

Water scarcity is one of the most critical global challenges of the 21st century. Despite significant public investments in water purification and treatment, a staggering volume of treated municipal water is lost due to leakages, pipeline fractures, broken public standposts, and uncontrolled water tank overflows before reaching consumers.

This project presents AquaReport, a beginner-friendly Flutter Web application designed to empower citizens to report water leakages directly to a central monitoring interface. The application directly supports United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 6 (SDG 6): Clean Water and Sanitation.

Built using the Flutter framework and Dart, the application implements a responsive Material Design 3 user interface with in-memory session state management. Key features include an interactive dashboard displaying live key performance indicators (Total, Pending, In Progress, Resolved), a validated reporting form with automated Complaint ID generation, a searchable and filterable complaints catalog, an ID tracking screen, and a demonstration control module enabling examiners to evaluate dynamic state synchronization. Automated unit and widget tests were developed to ensure system stability and layout responsiveness across desktop and mobile browsers.

2 Introduction

Clean water is essential for human life, sanitation, and sustainable human settlements. Sustainable Development Goal 6 established by the United Nations aims to guarantee universal access to safe water and sanitation by 2030. However, urban water distribution networks in developing and developed nations frequently experience severe physical water losses—termed in civil engineering as Non-Revenue Water (NRW).

The primary root cause of prolonged water loss is delayed detection. Municipal maintenance departments cannot monitor every street, alley, and pipeline junction round the clock. Citizens, conversely, witness water leakages daily in their neighborhoods but lack a quick, friction-free mechanism to report them without bureaucratic overhead, login barriers, or lengthy call-center waiting times.

The Clean Water and Sanitation: Water Leakage Reporting App was conceived as an academic software engineering project to solve this communication gap. By creating an intuitive, cross-platform web portal accessible from any standard browser, citizens can log an observed leakage in under one minute, receive a unique tracking identifier, and monitor resolution progress transparently.

3 Problem Statement

Traditional municipal water distribution networks suffer from four severe systemic deficiencies:

  1. Prolonged Leakage Lifespans: Underground pipe fissures and broken public taps frequently spew treated drinking water for days or weeks before routine utility inspections identify them.
  2. High Barrier to Reporting: Citizens often need to visit municipal offices in person or navigate complex telephone hotlines that lack ticket tracking, feedback, or accountability.
  3. Lack of Transparency: Once a citizen reports a complaint, there is rarely any feedback mechanism to verify if the issue has been inspected, dispatched, or resolved.
  4. Loss of Potable Water and Quality Degradation: Water gushing from high-pressure mains undermines road foundations, while depressurization during supply cuts allows contaminated groundwater to infiltrate drinking water distribution pipes, threatening public health.

4 Objectives

The core objectives of the AquaReport application are:

  • Simplified Reporting: Implement a clean, responsive web form requiring no login credentials, enabling any citizen to report a leak in under 60 seconds.
  • Automated Ticket Assignment: Generate an unambiguous, unique Complaint Reference ID (e.g., WLR-1009) for every submission to facilitate tracking.
  • Real-Time Dynamic Metrics: Provide an interactive dashboard aggregating complaints into Total, Pending, In Progress, and Resolved categories with instant updates upon status modifications.
  • Search and Status Filtering: Offer instant text search (by ID, locality, or citizen name) and chip-based status filtering.
  • Interactive Demonstration Controls: Incorporate testing controls directly within the complaint details screen to permit evaluators to test dynamic data propagation across screens without backend database setups.
  • SDG 6 Advocacy: Educate community members on water-saving facts, leak volume prevention, and civic conservation responsibility.

5 Existing System

Conventional municipal systems rely on physical registers at zonal offices, IVR telephone hotlines, or unstructured social media tags.

Disadvantages of the Existing System:
  • No Direct Incident Classification: Lacks standardized problem tags and structured severity indicators.
  • No Instant Tracking ID: Citizens do not receive a verifiable reference code to trace progress.
  • High Administrative Overhead: Manual entry is prone to record loss, duplication, and prolonged delays.
  • Zero Community Visibility: Neighbors cannot see existing reports, causing duplicate calls or complete bystander inaction.

6 Proposed System

The proposed AquaReport Flutter Web Application replaces disjointed reporting channels with a unified, lightweight, client-side digital portal.

Advantages of the Proposed System:
  • Zero Installation Barrier: Runs directly in any web browser on desktop, tablet, or mobile phone.
  • Structured Data Capture: Captures citizen name, location address, leakage category, problem narrative, observation date, and visual simulation tags.
  • Immediate Confirmation: Displays an instant confirmation dialog with the generated Complaint ID.
  • Transparent Progress Stepper: Visual timeline illustrating whether a complaint is Reported, In Progress, or Resolved.
  • Synchronized State: Status modifications on any individual complaint immediately recalculate and reflect on dashboard KPI cards and list screens via Flutter's reactive ChangeNotifier.

8 System Architecture & Modules

The system follows a clean, decoupled modular architecture where the user presentation layer communicates through a reactive singleton service down to an in-memory repository:

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE BLOCK DIAGRAM

Module Descriptions:

  1. Dashboard & Summary Module (home_screen.dart, summary_card.dart): Displays summary cards (Total, Pending, In Progress, Resolved), welcome hero banner, quick report action, recent reports feed, and UN SDG 6 impact facts.
  2. Incident Reporting Module (report_leakage_screen.dart): Manages the input form with field validators (GlobalKey<FormState>), date selector, problem category dropdown, optional photo tag selector, and success dialog with complaint ID generator.
  3. Complaints Registry Module (complaints_screen.dart, complaint_card.dart): Presents all active complaints in reverse chronological order, supporting real-time substring search across multiple fields and status-based filter chips.
  4. Complaint Tracking Module (track_complaint_screen.dart): Provides a specialized search tool allowing users to enter an ID (e.g., WLR-1001) or click demo chips to retrieve live status.
  5. Incident Inspection & Demo Module (complaint_details_screen.dart): Displays complete incident metadata, a visual progress stepper, and interactive status buttons for examiners to toggle status during academic demonstrations.
  6. Project Awareness Module (about_screen.dart): Details problem background, objectives, system workflow steps, and mapping to UN SDG Target 6.1 and 6.4.

10 Relevance to UN SDG 6

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 6 (SDG 6) seeks to "Ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all". AquaReport directly aligns with two critical international targets:

Target 6.1 — Universal and Equitable Access to Safe and Affordable Drinking Water

Clean, potable water requires extensive resources to collect, purify, treat with chemicals, and pump through municipal distribution mains. When high-pressure distribution lines rupture or public standposts fracture, treated drinking water is lost before reaching citizens.

More critically, when supply lines are depressurized during intermittent distribution schedules, cracked pipes allow surrounding non-potable groundwater, street runoff, and wastewater contaminants to seep into the distribution network. By providing an instantaneous reporting channel, AquaReport drastically truncates pipe repair latency, preserving drinking water purity and protecting public health.

Target 6.4 — Substantially Increase Water-Use Efficiency & Address Water Scarcity

Physical water loss within urban distribution networks—classified as Non-Revenue Water (NRW)—accounts for 30% to 50% of total treated water supply in many developing regions. A single standard pipeline break under typical mains pressure (3 bars) discharges approximately 10,000 to 15,000 liters of treated water per day.

Enabling community members to report incidents within minutes transforms thousands of citizens into proactive conservation monitors. This slashes detection cycles from weeks to mere hours, cumulatively saving millions of liters of treated water each year and significantly advancing water-use efficiency.

12 Limitations

While AquaReport fulfills all requirements of an academic software demonstration prototype, it possesses certain deliberate architectural simplifications:

  • Session-Bound Persistence: Complaint records are stored in client memory during the browser session. Refreshing the browser resets the data to the default sample dataset.
  • Absence of Backend Authentication: Citizen submission and status updating are open without role-based login to allow uncomplicated evaluation by examiners.
  • No External Cloud / Maps APIs: To preserve offline reliability and zero operating costs, external mapping APIs (such as Google Maps JS API) and cloud storage buckets were intentionally omitted in favor of self-contained text address capture and curated image tags.

13 Future Enhancements

  • Cloud Database Integration: Integration with Firebase Firestore or PostgreSQL for persistent cross-device synchronicity.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Dedicated citizen, maintenance contractor, and municipal commissioner portals with secure JWT authentication.
  • OpenStreetMap / GPS Integration: Auto-detecting coordinates using HTML5 Geolocation and rendering incident clusters on an interactive OpenStreetMap.
  • SMS / Email Alerts: Automated notifications sent to citizens when a reported leakage status shifts from Pending to In Progress to Resolved.
  • AI Leakage Image Classification: Automated image analysis using computer vision to estimate leak severity and priority from uploaded photos.

14 Conclusion

The Clean Water and Sanitation: Water Leakage Reporting App (AquaReport) successfully demonstrates how modern, cross-platform technologies like Flutter Web can be harnessed to solve pressing environmental and civic challenges. By providing an accessible, intuitive, and responsive interface, the project empowers everyday citizens to become active guardians of water conservation in their communities.

The application satisfies all academic software engineering standards: structured component architecture, clean separation of concerns, reactive state synchronization, Material 3 visual appeal, robust automated testing, and comprehensive documentation.
